Arrive
Volubilis — Roman Archaeological Site
Visit duration: approx. 1h 30 min · UNESCO World Heritage Site

Explore Morocco's most important Roman archaeological site, once a thriving city of 20,000 people at the western edge of the Roman Empire. Dating from the 3rd century BC, Volubilis is famous for its remarkably preserved mosaics, triumphal arch, and towering columns.

Triumphal Arch of Caracalla — the iconic gateway built in 217 AD, one of the best-preserved Roman arches in North Africa
Ancient Mosaics — spectacular floor mosaics depicting mythological scenes, preserved in situ for nearly 2,000 years
Roman Columns & Forum — the remains of the basilica, Capitol temple, and colonnaded streets of the ancient city centre
Panoramic views — sweeping countryside vistas over olive groves and the Zerhoun hills from the ruins
11:45
Arrive
Moulay Idriss Zerhoun
4 km from Volubilis · Sacred hilltop town · Zerhoun Mountains

Just 4 km from Volubilis, the sacred white hilltop town of Moulay Idriss Zerhoun clings dramatically to twin rocky peaks above the Zerhoun plain. This is Morocco's most revered pilgrimage site — home to the shrine of Moulay Idriss I, who founded the first Islamic dynasty in Morocco in the 8th century.

Shrine of Moulay Idriss I — final resting place of the founder of Morocco's first Islamic state (exterior visit)
Old Medina Stroll — winding alleys, whitewashed houses, and a peaceful atmosphere unlike anywhere else in Morocco
Panoramic Terrace — breathtaking view over the Zerhoun Mountains, the plains, and Volubilis in the distance
13:00
Lunch

Enjoy a relaxed lunch at a local restaurant. Your guide will recommend the best spots for authentic Moroccan cuisine — try the lamb tagine or couscous. Lunch is at your own expense but your guide will take you to honest, quality places.

14:30
Arrive
Meknes — Imperial City
Visit duration: approx. 2h 30 min · UNESCO World Heritage Site

Arrive in Meknes — the "Versailles of Morocco" — the grand imperial capital built by Sultan Moulay Ismail in the 17th century. The city's monumental gates, vast stables, and ancient medina were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

Bab Mansour — one of the most beautiful gates in North Africa, an extraordinary example of Moroccan Baroque architecture
Place el-Hedim — the lively main square of the medina, Meknes' answer to Djemaa el-Fna in Marrakech
Agdal Basin (Sahrij Souani) — the immense royal water reservoir and granary, a feat of 17th-century Moroccan engineering
Historic City Walls — the colossal ramparts built by Moulay Ismail, stretching for 40 km around the imperial city
Old Medina — a well-preserved UNESCO-listed medina with souks, craftsmen's quarters, and a distinctly local atmosphere
