About This Tour

Fes el Bali is the largest car-free urban area in the world and one of the best-preserved medieval medinas anywhere — a labyrinth of over 9,000 alleys that has barely changed in a thousand years. This tour is built for exactly that: a private driver-guide meets you at your hotel and walks you through the medina's essential landmarks on foot, with the car brought around for the hillside viewpoints that are too far to reach comfortably by walking. No long-distance driving, no departure city — just Fes, done properly.

Tour Itinerary

The half-day tour below runs roughly 09:00–13:00. Ask us about the full-day version ($79), which adds a relaxed lunch, deeper time in the artisan souks, and returns to the Merinid Tombs at golden hour for sunset over the medina.

09:00
Start
Meet Your Driver-Guide at Your Hotel
Hotel or riad pick-up in Fes · Tour begins on foot

Your private driver-guide meets you at your hotel or riad in the new city or the medina. From here, the tour is conducted mostly on foot inside Fes el Bali, since the medina's narrow alleys are closed to vehicles — the car and driver wait nearby or meet you again for the hillside stops.

09:15
Visit
Bab Boujloud — The Blue Gate
Main gateway into Fes el Bali · Photo stop

We enter the old medina through Bab Boujloud, the ornate horseshoe-arched gate decorated in blue and green zellige tilework — blue for Fes on the outward face, green for Islam on the inward face. It's the most photographed entrance to the medina and a natural starting point for exploring Fes el Bali on foot.

Blue & green tilework — the gate's two faces are decorated in different colors, one for each side of the medina
09:45
Visit
Al Quaraouiyine Mosque & University
Founded 859 AD · Exterior & courtyard glimpse (prayer hall closed to non-Muslims)

Founded in 859 AD by Fatima al-Fihri, Al Quaraouiyine is widely recognized — including by UNESCO and Guinness World Records — as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution in the world. As with all active mosques in Morocco, non-Muslims cannot enter the prayer hall, but you can admire the carved cedar eaves, the green-tiled minaret, and glimpse the tiled courtyard from the doorways along the surrounding alleys.

The world's oldest university — founded 859 AD, still teaching today
Minaret & courtyard views — glimpsed from the surrounding doorways and alleys
10:30
Visit
Nejjarine Museum of Wooden Arts & Crafts
Restored 18th-century fondouk · Entry ticket ~$4

Housed in a beautifully restored 18th-century fondouk — a historic caravanserai where traveling merchants once lodged and stored goods — the Nejjarine Museum displays traditional Moroccan woodworking tools, carved doors, musical instruments, and antique furniture around a stunning tiled courtyard. Even the building itself, with its carved cedar balconies, is worth the visit.

Restored fondouk architecture — carved cedar balconies around a courtyard, once a merchants' caravanserai
11:15
View
Chouara Tannery Viewpoint
Terrace view from a leather shop · Sprig of mint provided for the smell

The largest and oldest of Fes's three tanneries, Chouara has operated since the 11th century using the same stone dye pits and traditional methods largely unchanged since medieval times. The best view is from the terraces of the surrounding leather shops, looking down over the honeycomb of round stone vats filled with white lime, brown tannin, and vivid colored dyes — genuinely one of the most striking sights in Morocco.

Stone dye pits — dozens of circular vats in a honeycomb pattern, filled with natural dyes
Working tannery — one of Morocco's oldest continuously operating industries, since the 11th century
12:00
Drive
Royal Palace — Golden Doors
Private car transfer · Exterior only, palace grounds are closed to the public

Your driver-guide brings the car around for a short transfer to the Royal Palace (Dar al-Makhzen) in the Fes Jdid district. The palace itself is not open to visitors, but its monumental brass-covered doors — seven arched gates framed in intricate zellige tilework and calligraphy — are a landmark in their own right and a favorite photo stop.

12:30
View
Merinid Tombs & Borj Nord — Panoramic Viewpoint
Hilltop north of the medina · The single best panorama of Fes

The tour's grand finale: the ruined 14th-century Merinid Tombs and the nearby Borj Nord fortress sit on a hilltop directly above Fes el Bali, offering an unbroken panoramic view of the entire medina spilling down the valley below — minarets, rooftops, and the distant hum of the city. On the full-day version of this tour, we return here at golden hour for sunset, when the light turns the medina gold.

Full medina panorama — the single best view over the rooftops of Fes el Bali
14th-century ruins — the Merinid royal necropolis, evocative even in its weathered state
13:00
End
Half-Day Tour Ends · Or Continue on the Full-Day Option
Drop-off at your hotel · Full-day guests continue with lunch & more souk time

Half-day guests are dropped back at their hotel around 13:00. If you've booked the full-day option ($79), we instead break for lunch, spend the afternoon exploring deeper into the artisan souks (weavers, dyers, metalworkers, and woodcarvers), and finish the day back at the Merinid Tombs for sunset over the medina, with drop-off around 19:00.
